## 1. Dossier-to-live medium correspondence is now validated

### OBSERVED

In `B010-004`, `B010-005` and `B010-012`, live Vulture appears as a jagged brown hand-drawn / etched mass inside comparatively smooth painterly/3D Earth-65 space.

### INFERRED

The dossier did not merely contain decorative concept art. It previewed a **character-owned rendering medium** that persists when the character enters the live scene.

```text

→ retain recognizable medium signature
→ live character arrival
→ preserve medium boundary against host world
```

## 2. Entity-medium ownership, not a global mixed-media filter

### OBSERVED

At `B010-005`, several rendering systems coexist at once:

- painterly / dimensional blue-violet arena;
- luminous translucent magenta-white event shards;
- tiny clean Gwen figure;
- large brown etched/sketched Vulture wing.

The Vulture medium does not spread across Gwen, the arena or the whole frame.

### INFERRED

Mixed media is operating as **entity / layer ownership**. Medium contrast itself contributes to threat authority.

### GENERATIVE RULE

Assign medium per entity or representational layer before assigning any global style treatment.

Add:

- `entity_medium_ownership`
- `medium_boundary_persistence`
- `threat_authority_by_medium`
- `rendering_medium_leakage` QA

**Hard failure:** propagating Vulture's hatch/etched grammar across unrelated surfaces as a generic comic filter.

## 3. Force-vector alignment carries impact readability

### OBSERVED

At `B010-006`, Gwen's body forms a long diagonal while debris shards, basketballs and the event-light field all agree on the displacement direction. The following `B010-007` landing converts that travel vector into foreshortened bodily compression.

### INFERRED

Impact is clearer when force is distributed coherently across several visible systems. Motion blur is secondary.

### GENERATIVE RULE

```text
dominant force vector
→ body pose axis
→ secondary-object displacement
→ debris direction
→ event-light origin
→ landing compression
```

Add: `force_vector_alignment`, `secondary_object_displacement`, `impact_pose_axis`, `landing_compression`.

**Hard failure:** blur-only action where body, debris and prop vectors disagree.

## 4. Recovery can be carried by micro-posture, not global FX

### OBSERVED

`B010-009–010` hold Gwen low to the ground. The transition into recovery is small: torso/hood lift and one supporting arm change geometry while palette and environmental rendering remain largely held.

### INFERRED

Agency can return through a small posture release. The sequence does not need escalating effects to announce resolve.

### GENERATIVE RULE

When posture already carries the state change, hold palette, texture and global FX. Protect the active body geometry instead.

## 5. Post-impact visual silence as a description-density reset

### OBSERVED

`B010-011` nearly eliminates readable subject/object information: broad navy planes dominate with only a tiny green accent. It follows dense impact/recovery material and immediately precedes the high-specificity Vulture re-entry in `B010-012`.

### INFERRED

Visual silence is not limited to calm or emotional dialogue scenes. It can act as an **action-rhythm reset**, clearing attention before threat specificity returns.

### GENERATIVE RULE

```text
high information density
→ impact / aftermath
→ description-density collapse
→ minimal orientation anchor
→ high-specificity re-entry
```

Add: `description_density_reset`, `post_impact_visual_silence`, `reentry_contrast_gain`.

**Hard failure:** keeping saturation, edge density, object density and FX continuously maximal throughout an action sequence.
