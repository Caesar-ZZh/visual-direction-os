## Change point 1 — Character access

### B001-001

**OBSERVED**

- Gwen occupies the primary readable character territory.
- City / architecture behind her are heavily simplified into broad rose, peach and lilac fields.
- Face and hair retain stronger structure than the environment.
- The city does not behave like a realistically illuminated photo background.

**INFERRED**

The scene protects Gwen through a small set of graphic identity channels while permitting environmental physical description to collapse.

**Production implication**

A Gwen-like emotional rendering should simplify the environment *before* applying decorative watercolor/print texture.

## Change point 2 — Foreground power distance

### B001-004

This is currently the most important correction to the original Character/World theory.

**OBSERVED**

- George Stacy becomes an oversized dark/purple foreground mass.
- Gwen is much smaller and pushed into a bright rear plane.
- Foreground occlusion and scale asymmetry dominate the read.
- The relationship is legible even if color texture is mentally removed.

**INFERRED**

Relationship boundary is not only an Edge × Color phenomenon. **Scale, occlusion, subject territory and plane separation** can carry relational authority more directly.

**GENERATIVE RULE**

For relationship-pressure scenes, composition must be allowed to depart from source photography. Preserve identity anchors, not necessarily original camera geometry.

## Change point 3 — Profile / color separation

### B001-008

**OBSERVED**

- Gwen's skin/face is rendered in cool blue-violet rather than fixed natural skin color.
- Her profile remains readable through selected facial landmarks.
- Background / father occupy a distinct warm, pale plane.
- Architecture becomes vertical painterly fields rather than fully described surfaces.

**INFERRED**

Earth-65 emotional color can override physically local color, including skin, while readability is preserved through shape and selective edge structure.

**GENERATIVE RULE**

Do not force natural skin tone when the emotional field has narrative authority. Preserve facial-plane readability, not realistic complexion.

## Change point 4 — Wide environmental field

### B001-011

**OBSERVED**

- Large high-key peach/rose/yellow territories dominate the room.
- Gwen becomes a compact cobalt/indigo vertical anchor.
- Negative space and environmental color carry substantial emotional weight.
- Physical architectural description is heavily reduced.

**INFERRED**

The world itself carries the emotional state. The character need not occupy a portrait-sized portion of the frame for the scene to remain character-centric.

**GENERATIVE RULE**

Emotionally directed stylization may reduce character screen area and increase environmental territory when the world grammar is the stronger emotional carrier.

## Rendering findings from this batch

### What is *not* universal here

- no evidence that halftone must be dominant;
- no evidence that hatching must be dominant;
- no need for neon;
- no global chromatic aberration;
- no need for dense high-frequency texture;
- no need to retain natural object/skin colors.

### What is strongly visible

- low-frequency painterly fields;
- high Reality Suppression;
- Controlled Flattening;
- architectural Detail Suppression;
- selective character hard edges;
- relationship-driven scale and occlusion;
- high-key world + concentrated character anchor;
- psychological color stronger than physical lighting description.

## Implication for the old knowledge base

Old shorthand:

```text

```

Should evolve toward:

```text

```

`Boundary` remains useful as a narrative concept, but it is **not a rendering recipe**.
